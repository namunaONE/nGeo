## 1. Purpose

v1.0 is a complete, frozen administrative atlas: 7 provinces, 77 districts and 753 local levels, with three print sheets and an offline web app. v1.1 is additive. It adds the national conservation estate and the high peaks as **reference layers** that sit alongside — not inside — the administrative hierarchy.

The design problem is not simply “add more polygons”. It is to make three different kinds of information legible at once without confusing them:

1. **Administrative units** — legal tiers with codes, counts and a province → district → local hierarchy.
2. **Protected areas and buffer zones** — management designations that cut across districts and provinces.
3. **Peaks** — point locations, not areas.

The atlas must therefore keep administrative and non-administrative information separate in the data model, the layer controls, the legend, the search results, the popups and the print sheets. A reader must never be able to mistake a national park for a local level, a buffer zone for a district, or a peak symbol for a summit polygon.

### Success criteria

- All required layers are present, bilingual and attributable.
- The administrative counts and geometry remain exactly as v1.0.
- Peaks are points only; no peak polygon exists anywhere in the data, web app or print output.
- Buffer zones and the provincial Badimalika area are visibly distinct from federal core protected areas.
- Every map and data product carries an open-data, non-survey-grade, non-legal-boundary notice.
- The web app remains fully offline and the print set remains print-ready.
- No source licence is violated and no boundary is invented.

### 2.2 Out of scope

- Ward-level geometry or ward tables (Phase 2).
- Official Survey Department boundary geometry, or any edition based on it.
- WDPA geometry in the redistributable offline bundle.
- Ramsar sites, UNESCO sites, forest areas, community forests, hunting blocks or tourism routes as separate layers.
- Climbing permits, expedition data, route lines, camps or safety information.
- Real-time data, external basemaps or online tiles.
- Any change to the v1.0 province, district or local-level geometry, names, codes or counts.
- Any legal, cadastral or survey-grade determination.

### 2.3 Relationship to v1.0

v1.0 remains the administrative base. v1.1 adds files and layers; it does not rebuild the administrative hierarchy. The existing `data/provinces.geojson`, `data/districts.geojson` and `data/local_levels.geojson` are treated as read-only inputs. `summary.json` may gain a `reference` block, but its administrative counts must not change.

Before any work, snapshot the frozen v1.0 delivery (there is no git repository):

```text
maps/_archive/pilot-v1.0-2026-10-26/
```

or an equivalent zip archive outside the active `maps/pilot` tree. This protects the frozen edition and gives QA a clean comparison baseline.

### 3.3 The four gap polygons

The four gaps are real artefacts of dissolving the local-level geometry, not protected-area features. The Shivapuri gap near `27.824, 85.372` is likely explained by Shivapuri Nagarjun National Park, but this must be verified, not assumed.

**Rule:** keep the gap geometry and its “no local-level data” meaning. When the protected-area layer is added, audit each gap against the new polygons:

- If a gap is fully covered by a protected area, keep the gap feature but restyle or annotate it as “no local-level data within a protected area”.
- If it is not fully covered, retain the existing treatment.
- Do not delete gap geometry merely because a reference polygon now covers it.
- Do not alter the v1.0 administrative geometry to close a gap.

### 4.4 Peaks

#### The official 14

The atlas should present both classes rather than adjudicate the dispute.

Height and status notes:

- Heights vary by source. Use Nepal's official figures for the 14 table, and record the source and source date.
- The eight main peaks are the internationally recognised eight-thousanders in Nepal.
- The six additional summits are subsidiary summits in the Kangchenjunga and Lhotse massifs. They are not separate eight-thousander mountains in the UIAA sense.
- Do not describe the 14 as “internationally recognised”. Use “Nepal's official 2025 peak profile lists 14”.

#### Curated 7,000 m list

The starting point is the 49-row Wikipedia list of named Nepal peaks at or above 7,000 m, cross-checked with Wikidata coordinates and labels and with the Himalayan Database peak list.

Proposed editorial rules:

1. The summit is in Nepal, or on a Nepal border, with coordinates inside the national polygon or within a small border tolerance.
2. It is a named independent summit, not a subsidiary point or forepeak of a higher peak.
3. Prominence is ≥ 300 m where a prominence figure exists. If prominence is unknown, the peak may be included only if it has a distinct name, a distinct mapped summit and no evidence of being a subsidiary point.
4. The eight main eight-thousanders and the six official additional summits are classified separately and are not repeated in the 7,000 m list.
5. One feature per summit. Alternate names become aliases, not duplicate features.
6. Every peak has an English name and a Nepali name. If a Nepali name is not attested, use a reviewed transliteration and record `name_np_status: transliterated`; do not machine-translate.
7. Border peaks are flagged `nepal_share: border` and list the neighbouring countries. They are not described as wholly inside Nepal.
8. A peak is shown only as a labelled point symbol. No buffers, viewsheds or summit polygons.

Applying rules 1–5 to the current 49-row list gives **37 candidates**. The count may change slightly after the border and Nepali-name audit; freeze it at the data review gate.

The candidate list is in Appendix B. Known data issues to resolve:

- **Dhaulagiri VI** currently carries Dhaulagiri I's coordinates in the source table; correct it.
- **Nepal Peak** may be on the Nepal–India border rather than wholly inside Nepal; verify.
- **Pabil / Ganesh II / Ganesh III** naming is inconsistent across sources; resolve to one summit per feature.
- Several peaks have no Nepali label in Wikidata. These need a reviewed source, not transliteration by default.
- The official six additional summits are absent from the Wikipedia 7,000 m table except Yalung Kang; they must come from the official 14 table.

## 5. Source and licensing strategy

### 5.1 Decision tree

Work down this list; stop at the first source that is both credible and legally usable.

1. **DNPWC written permission + official boundary package.**
   - Request the current 21 core areas and 13 buffer zones as GeoJSON/SHP, plus a dated attribute table.
   - Ask specifically about Chhayanath and any post-2025 changes.
   - Record the permission in `data/reference/sources.csv`.
   - This is the only route that can support the word “official” for the protected-area geometry.

2. **OSM / Overpass extract, if ODbL is acceptable.**
   - Query relations and ways with `boundary=protected_area`, `protect_class`, `protection_title`, `operator=DNPWC`.
   - Time-box the audit to half a day. Earlier attempts returned 406, 502 and TLS errors; retry with POST, a descriptive `User-Agent`, and an alternate endpoint.
   - ODbL is compatible with an open atlas only if the combined geodata bundle is relicensed to ODbL and the attribution/share-alike notice is updated. Do not mix ODbL geometry into a CC BY 4.0 bundle without that decision.
   - Use OSM for geometry only if it agrees with DNPWC published areas and designations; otherwise use it as a QA reference, not a shipping source.

3. **`stepps00/Protected-Areas-Data-Nepal`, CC BY 4.0.**
   - A 2023, 20-feature shapefile. It predates Chhayanath and Badimalika and combines some parks and buffer zones.
   - Usable only as a clearly labelled **indicative fallback** for older core areas.
   - Do not present it as current, authoritative, or complete.
   - If used, separate core and buffer features, reconcile names and areas, and add the missing 2025/2026 designations as non-spatial records.

4. **WDPA / Protected Planet.**
   - The WDPA licence restricts commercial use and redistribution. An offline `atlas.js` that can be downloaded is redistribution.
   - Use WDPA for **verification only**, not as a shipped geometry source, unless written permission is obtained.
   - If permission is obtained, record the current version, attribution and Protected Planet link.

5. **No source.**
   - Publish a non-spatial record. Do not trace a boundary from a copyrighted map, do not generate a polygon with a model, and do not reuse the 10-feature draft.

**Official Survey Department geometry** is a separate track. It is not part of v1.1 and must not be mixed into an open-data edition without explicit permission.

### 5.2 Licence matrix

| Source | Licence | Can it ship in the offline bundle? | Intended use |
|---|---|---|---|

### 5.3 Attribution strings

Use short, consistent strings in the web attribution control, popups, print footers and README.

**If DNPWC permission is obtained:**

> Protected areas: Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation (DNPWC), [year], used with permission. Boundaries: Open Knowledge Nepal, LocalBoundaries (CC BY 4.0). Peaks: Wikidata / Wikipedia / Himalayan Database, compiled for this atlas.

**If the CC BY 4.0 fallback is used:**

> Protected areas: `stepps00/Protected-Areas-Data-Nepal` (CC BY 4.0), 2023, indicative only; 2025–2026 designations shown as records without boundaries. Boundaries: Open Knowledge Nepal, LocalBoundaries (CC BY 4.0). Peaks: Wikidata / Wikipedia / Himalayan Database.

**If OSM geometry is used:**

> Protected-area geometry © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. The geodata bundle for this edition is distributed under ODbL 1.0. Boundaries: Open Knowledge Nepal, LocalBoundaries (CC BY 4.0).

Never omit the “not survey-grade, not a legal boundary” sentence, even when the geometry is official.

### 5.4 What must not ship

- WDPA geometry without permission.
- OSM geometry in a bundle still labelled CC BY 4.0.
- The existing 10-feature draft.
- A peak polygon, a summit buffer, or a “protected area” derived from a peak location.
- A Chhayanath or Badimalika polygon created by tracing a news graphic or estimating from a description.
- Any statement that the atlas is a legal, cadastral or survey-grade boundary determination.

Peaks are **Point** features. The validator rejects any `Polygon` or `MultiPolygon` in `peaks.geojson`.

### 6.4 Identifiers and names

- Use lowercase ASCII slugs, not sequential codes: `pa-np-chitwan`, `pa-ca-annapurna`, `pa-wr-koshi-tappu`, `pa-hr-dhorpatan`, `pa-ca-badimalika`, `bz-chitwan`, `pk-everest`.
- Slugs must be stable across editions. Do not renumber when a new area is added.
- Keep old names as aliases (`Parsa Wildlife Reserve`, `Chitawan`) so search still works.
- `name_np` is mandatory. If a Nepali name cannot be attested, use a reviewed transliteration and set `name_np_status: transliterated` in the master table and data README. Do not leave the field empty.
- Do not machine-translate protected-area names or peak names. Use DNPWC, Wikidata, Wikipedia/newiki, or a named Nepali reviewer.

### 6.5 Areas: published vs computed

Store both `area_published_km2` and `area_geom_km2`.

- Display `area_published_km2` in popups and print, with the source date.
- Use `area_geom_km2` only for QA and for a “computed from geometry” note when no published figure exists.
- If the difference is greater than 15%, warn; greater than 30%, fail the build unless the row has an explicit `area_note` explaining why.
- Never label a computed area as “official”.

This distinction resolves most of the apparent errors in the old draft: its areas were computed from incomplete or clipped geometry, not copied from published tables.

### 6.6 Validation rules

`build/prepare_reference.js` must fail the build on any of the following:

- Wrong feature count for a layer (21 federal core, 13 buffer zones, optional 1 provincial, frozen peak count).
- Duplicate or missing IDs; IDs not matching `^[a-z0-9-]+$`.
- Empty `name_en` or `name_np`; `name_np` with no Devanagari characters.
- Geometry type mismatch: areas must be Polygon/MultiPolygon; peaks must be Point.
- Coordinates outside Nepal's bounding box (approximately 80–89°E, 26–31°N), except a documented border tolerance for peaks.
- Any peak polygon.
- Missing `source_id`, `source_date` or `license`.
- A buffer zone without a valid `parent_pa_id`.
- Core-area overlap greater than 0.5% of the smaller polygon.
- Buffer-zone overlap with its parent core greater than 1% of the buffer area, unless `geometry_note` records the issue and the rendering order hides the overlap.
- Duplicate names within a layer without an alias/disambiguation note.
- `review_status` other than `approved` in a release build.

The script writes a machine-readable report:

```text
build/reference_qa.json
```

with counts, area comparisons, overlaps, missing names and label-anchor checks. The report is a release artefact.

### 6.7 Build pipeline

```text
1. import_reference.js       (one-off: SHP/GeoJSON -> normalised GeoJSON)
2. prepare_reference.js      (validate, compute areas, label points, simplify web)
3. make_web_data.js          (bundle atlas.js)
4. make_national.js
5. make_bagmati.js
6. make_kathmandu.js
7. make_peaks.js
8. PDF export via Edge headless
9. PDFium + Playwright QA
```

`prepare_reference.js` writes the simplified web geometry to `web/data/protected_areas_web.geojson` and `web/data/buffer_zones_web.geojson`. Print builders read the full-resolution `data/*.geojson`.

Simplification target: 5% tolerance, matching the existing web local-level treatment. The protected-area boundary is a reference line, not a legal edge; 5% is acceptable for the web if the print sheets use full resolution. Do not apply 5% blindly to small areas: preserve polygons below roughly 50 km² (for example Krishnasar) at full resolution or use a smaller tolerance, and verify that no polygon collapses or self-intersects. Measure the bundle size after each build.

## 7. Web implementation

### 7.1 Panes and z-order

The current map creates panes for graticule, province, district, local, district lines, gaps and capital. Add reference panes in the correct order so administrative lines remain crisp and reference fills never sit on top of them.

| Pane | z-index | Contents |
|---|---:|---|
| `graticulePane` | 300 | Graticule |
| `provincePane` | 310 | Province fills |
| `districtPane` | 320 | District fills |
| `localPane` | 330 | Local-level fills |
| `gapPane` | 331 | “No local-level data” polygons |
| `protectedAreaPane` | 332 | Core protected-area wash |
| `bufferZonePane` | 333 | Buffer-zone hatch |
| `districtLinePane` | 335 | District outlines |
| `peakPane` | 650 | Peak symbols |
| `peakLabelPane` | 655 | Peak labels |
| `capitalPane` | 660 | National capital |

Why this order:

- Protected-area fills sit above local fills but below district outlines, so the administrative mesh stays readable.
- Buffer zones sit below core areas, so a shared edge reads as core-on-top.
- Peak symbols sit above every polygon, because a point symbol must not be hidden by a fill.
- Peak labels sit above peak symbols and below the capital marker.

### 7.2 Visual encoding

The reference layers must look like reference information, not like another administrative tier.

| Provincial protected area | Same fill, opacity 0.18 | Teal `#3F7F78`, 1.1 px, dotted `2 3` | Small caps + “PROVINCIAL” badge | On if included |
| Buffer zone | 45° hatch `#B08A4E` at 0.15, no solid fill | `#A98B4F`, 0.9 px, dashed `3 3` | Small caps, ochre, lower priority | Off |
| 8,000 m main | Filled dark triangle `#1F2937` | Hairline halo | Name + Nepali + elevation, priority 1 | On |
| Official additional 8,000 m | Open triangle, dark outline | Hairline halo | Name + “official additional” tag, priority 2 | On |
| 7,000 m | Small grey-ink triangle `#4B5563` | Hairline halo | Name + elevation, priority 3 | On at z ≥ 8 |

Rules:

### 7.6 Labels

The current label engine places one administrative kind at a time (`province`, `district`, `local`), with collision avoidance and a cap of 90 local labels. Reference labels need a combined pass.

Recommended approach:

1. In `refreshLabels()`, build the administrative candidate list as today.
2. Build a reference candidate list:
   - Protected areas: only if the layer is on; label anchors from `label_lon/lat`; priority by published area.
   - Buffer zones: only if the layer is on; only at z ≥ 8.5 and only for zones above a minimum area (e.g. 250 km²); priority lower than core areas.
   - Peaks: if the peaks layer and peak labels are on; national view shows the 14 eight-thousanders; z ≥ 8 shows the 7,000 m set; z ≥ 9.5 shows all.
3. Place peak labels first into the `occupied` array, because they are small and must remain attached to their symbols; then place protected-area labels; then administrative labels. If a label cannot be placed, drop it rather than overlap.
4. On narrow screens (`compact-labels`), reduce the peak set to main 8 + official 6 below z 9.5.

```text
bufferZone    { p: 8.0, pw: 600, s: 7.0, sw: 600, pl: 11, sl: 9 }
```

```html
<div class="map-label label-peak">

  <span class="lb-secondary np">सगरमाथा</span>
  <span class="lb-elev">8,848.86 m</span>
```

## 9. Bilingual and editorial rules

### 9.1 Names

- `name_en` and `name_np` are mandatory for every feature.
- Nepali names come from DNPWC, Wikidata `ne` labels, Nepali Wikipedia, or a named reviewer. Do not machine-translate.
- If no attested Nepali name exists, use a reviewed transliteration and record `name_np_status: transliterated`.
- Keep old names and alternate spellings as aliases: `Parsa Wildlife Reserve`, `Chitawan`, `Mount Everest`, `Chomolungma`.
- In the `ne` interface, Nepali is primary and English secondary. In `en`, English is primary and Nepali secondary. This matches v1.0.
- Every print sheet carries a bilingual title, legend and accuracy note, not only bilingual feature names. Every new web UI string has an `en` and a `ne` entry in the `I18N` table; do not leave a new control English-only.

### 9.2 Numbers and units

- Web: localise digits to Devanagari in `ne` using the existing `formatNum()`.
- Print: use Latin digits for elevations and areas in both languages, to avoid ambiguity in technical figures; use Devanagari for Nepali names and prose.
- Use `km²` and `m` consistently. Nepali prose may use `किमी²` and `मि`.
- Do not mix hectares, square kilometres and acres in the same table.

### 9.3 Status wording

Use precise, defensible language:

- “Nepal's official 2025 peak profile lists 14 peaks above 8,000 m.”
- “UIAA/international recognition remains eight main eight-thousanders.”
- “The Cabinet had not approved the 14-peak list; UIAA recognition is pending.”
- “Provincial conservation area — not part of the federal DNPWC network.”
- “Buffer zone — not a core protected area.”
- “Indicative extent — not a legal boundary.”
- “Point symbol — no summit polygon.”

Avoid:

- “Official boundary” unless DNPWC/Survey Department permission is documented.
- “Survey-grade”, “cadastral”, “legal boundary”.
- “Nepal has 14 eight-thousanders” without the official/UIAA distinction.
- “All protected areas” when the layer is incomplete.
- “Exact area” for a geometry-derived figure.

### 9.4 Dates and versions

- Show the edition date and source date in the web footer and print footer.
- Use `review_status: approved` only after the data review gate.
- Record the DNPWC/WDPA/OSM access date in `sources.csv`.
- If a designation changes during the build, stop and re-run the master table; do not patch the generated GeoJSON.

## 14. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| No authoritative, openly licensed protected-area geometry | High | High | Send DNPWC request immediately; use `stepps00` as a labelled fallback; publish table-only records for missing designations; do not fabricate. |
| WDPA/OSM licence contamination | Medium | High | Licence matrix; no WDPA in the bundle; OSM only with an ODbL relicense decision; legal review before release. |
| Area figures do not reconcile | High | Medium | Store published and computed areas separately; publish footnotes; freeze only reconciled figures; show “under review” where needed. |
| Peak list dispute (8 vs 14; official status) | Medium | Medium | Dual classification; precise wording; source dates; do not adjudicate. |
| Label clutter, especially on the national sheet | High | Medium | Dedicated sheet 04; selective national labels; numbered keys; `Labels` collision tests; PDFium visual QA. |
| Bundle size/performance | Medium | Low | 5% simplification; measure; target < 2.0 MB; no external assets. |
| DNPWC site instability (503s) | High | Low | Use archived pages, written requests, and cached copies; record access dates. |
| No git; accidental overwrite of v1.0 | Medium | High | Snapshot and manifest before work; treat v1.0 files as read-only. |
| Schedule slip from source permission | High | Medium | Parallelise data/table work with the permission request; define the reduced scope; do not promise a release date before G1. |
| Double counting core + buffer + provincial | Medium | High | Separate counts, separate totals, separate legend groups; never sum without a footnote. |
| Gap polygons misread as protected areas | Medium | Low | Keep gap semantics; audit against the PA layer; annotate rather than delete. |
| Chhayanath/Badimalika status or area changes | Medium | Medium | Date-stamp every record; `review_status`; re-check before release. |
| Peak coordinates inaccurate or duplicated | Medium | Medium | Cross-check Wikidata, Wikipedia and Himalayan Database; fix Dhaulagiri VI; record coordinate accuracy. |
| Nepali names missing or inconsistent | High | Medium | Nepali reviewer; transliteration flag; no machine translation; aliases for alternate spellings. |
